# Route memory with surprise nudging: adversarial sweep of patents, open-source projects, blogs and 2024-2026 preprints

Sweep date: 2026-11-24. Method: about 20 web searches and 10 page fetches. Verification levels are marked per item: **[fetched]** = I opened the primary page this session; **[snippet]** = seen only in a search-result summary, not opened; **[memory]** = from my own background knowledge, not re-verified this session (treat as a lead, not a fact).

Headline: **no item found matches the whole combination**, and no item found matches pairing (A) or the exact form of pairing (B). Pairings (C) and (D) have partial neighbours. The patent part of the sweep is weak: the search tool does not run Google Patents / USPTO / Espacenet / Patentscope queries natively, so "not found" for patents means "not surfaced by web search", not "cleared".

## Does anything match the WHOLE combination (binary MLP-gate route keys + carried alignment kernel + stored (told - predicted) added to logits, frozen model, no training)?

### Takeaway
Not found. The nearest whole-system neighbours each share two or three of the four parts and differ on the rest; the closest in spirit are kNN-LM/RetoMaton (stored pass, similarity-weighted logit-side effect, pointer following), untrained fast-weight SGD on a frozen Qwen3 (arXiv 2610.33025), and the activation-steering memory of arXiv 2610.14958.

### Cited Findings
- **kNN-LM (Khandelwal et al., arXiv 1911.00172)**: datastore keys are hidden-state vectors, values are the next tokens; the kNN distribution is interpolated with the model distribution by a scalar; no training of the LM. Overlap: one forward pass to write, per-token store of the "told" token, frozen model. Difference: dense hidden-state keys (not binary gate patterns), stores the target only (no stored model prediction, no error), normalised mixture (the method here is explicitly an un-normalised additive log-score nudge), independent per-token lookups (no carried alignment). **[snippet, multiple searches]** — [arXiv 1911.00172](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.00172)
- **RetoMaton (Alon et al., ICML 2022, arXiv 2201.12431)**: builds a weighted finite automaton over the kNN-LM datastore using "saved pointers between consecutive entries" and clustering; transition weights depend on the distance between current and stored hidden states; saves up to 83% of kNN searches. Overlap: follows the stored sequence forward from a previous match (the closest known thing to "carry the match to the next stored place"). Difference: discrete pointer following / automaton states with hard traversal, not the soft recurrence P_t[n] = sim^scale * (1 + P_(t-1)[n-1]) over all stored places; still targets-only and an interpolated mixture; dense keys. **[snippet + ICML slides snippet]** — [arXiv 2201.12431](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.12431), [ICML slides](https://ICML.cc/media/icml-2022/Slides/16305.pdf)
- **"Can Language Models Learn While They Think? Testing Gradient Adaptation Across Architectures" (Surya Saka, arXiv 2610.33025, 10 Oct 2026)**: no training; one SGD step per 128-token chunk on the MLP output projections (`down_proj`) of the last quarter of blocks of otherwise frozen models, including Qwen3-1.7B and Qwen3-4B; loss falls 5-8% on books and court opinions for softmax-attention models; on eight retrieval/long-context benchmarks the fast weights recover essentially none of the accuracy lost to truncation; writing facts with next-token loss "stored the training format more than the fact" and degraded GSM8K. The paper says retrieval would need "a key-to-value binding that a handful of SGD steps on a late projection does not form." Overlap: frozen pretrained Qwen3, inference-time, error-driven (gradient of next-token loss), the "learn from what it is told and drift" intent. Difference: real weight updates in primal form (weights are changed, then reset per document), no stored per-token memory, no explicit key, no kernel, no carried alignment, not the LM head. This is the most direct **negative-result** neighbour and a reviewer would cite it against the claim. **[fetched]** — [arXiv 2610.33025](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.33025)
- **"What Survives When Activation Steering Is Used as Memory" (Vedant Jumle, arXiv 2610.14958, 10 Oct 2026, v2 19 Oct 2026)**: training-free activation memory beside a frozen LLM; stores the shift an experience causes in late-layer states as a steering vector, filed under a whitened key for the conversation's gist, added back to activations in a later session only when the gist matches; reports facts end up "stored as the word to say, in the last layers only". Overlap: frozen, training-free, activation recordings as memory, not RAG. Difference: dense whitened gist key (not binary neuron patterns), gated on a match (the method here is never gated), acts on activations not logits, the abstract states no (told - predicted) term, per-conversation not per-token. **[fetched, abstract only]** — [arXiv 2610.14958](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14958)
- **Prometheus Mind (Mark Wind, arXiv 2601.15324, 18 Jan 2026)**: retrofits memory to a frozen Qwen3-4B with eleven trained modular adapters, "fully reversible by removing the adapters"; uses lm_head weight rows as the value mapping "requiring no training". Overlap: frozen Qwen3, output-side value mapping. Difference: adapters are trained; no error term; keys from a trained encoder. **[fetched, abstract only]** — [arXiv 2601.15324](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15324)
- **Meta-Learning Fast Weight Language Models (Clark, Guu, Chang, Pasupat, Hinton, Norouzi, arXiv 2212.02475, 5 Dec 2022)**: Fast Weight Layers express dynamic-evaluation gradient updates as linear attention on top of a transformer. Overlap: this is the published "dual form of a last-layer gradient step as attention over earlier tokens" for language models, i.e. the same algebra as step 3. Difference: FWLs "can also be applied at training time so the model learns to make good use of gradient updates" (meta-learned); keys are the dense final hidden states inside the current sequence; no persistent cross-prompt store, no binary keys, no carried alignment. **[fetched, abstract only]** — [arXiv 2212.02475](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.02475)

### Inferences
- The whole combination appears to be unpublished as of this sweep, but each ingredient except (A) has a named ancestor, so the defensible novelty claim is the combination plus the binary-route key and the ungated carried kernel, not "learning without weight updates".
- The algebra of step 3 (one output-layer gradient step per stored token, kept in dual form) is known (FWL 2022); the novelty there is only that it is persisted across prompts on an untrained frozen model with a non-hidden-state kernel.
- arXiv 2610.33025 is the paper most likely to be raised as "this was tried and does not bind facts"; the difference to stress is explicit keyed dual-form storage versus a few primal SGD steps.

### Gaps
- Full texts of 2610.14958, 2601.15324 and 2212.02475 were not read (abstract pages only), so exact key/injection details could differ from the abstracts.
- Earlier researchers in this effort presumably cover the classics (neural cache, dynamic evaluation, kNN-LM follow-ups); those were not re-surveyed here.

## Pairing (A): binary neuron on/off activation patterns as keys for a language-model memory

### Takeaway
Not found. Gate-pattern similarity exists as theory (Neural Path Kernel) and as a signal for other tasks (pre-training-data detection), and FFN-as-key-value-memory exists as interpretation, but nothing surfaced uses the binary on/off pattern of all MLP units as the retrieval key of an inference-time LM memory.

### Cited Findings
- **Neural Path Features and Neural Path Kernel (arXiv 2006.10529, NeurIPS 2020)**: treats ReLUs as gates that are on or off and defines a kernel from the gate patterns, arguing it "characterises the information stored in the gates of a DNN". Overlap: this is the theoretical ancestor of "share of units with equal on/off state as a kernel between two inputs". Difference: a theory of trained ReLU networks' generalisation; no language model, no memory, no retrieval. (The search summary's author attribution looked wrong, so authors are not stated here.) **[snippet]** — [arXiv 2006.10529](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10529)
- **Identifying Pre-training Data in LLMs: A Neuron Activation-Based Detection Framework (arXiv 2507.16414)**: uses sets of activated neurons as a similarity signal, for membership/pre-training-data detection. Overlap: neuron activation sets compared across texts on an LLM. Difference: detection, not memory or generation. **[snippet]** — [arXiv 2507.16414](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.16414)
- **Memory Retrieval and Consolidation in Large Language Models (arXiv 2510.08203)**: interprets FFN activations as key-value memory (d_m pairs of key and value vectors). Interpretation of the weights, not an added memory keyed by gate patterns. **[snippet]** — [arXiv 2510.08203](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.08203)
- **PaceLLM (arXiv 2506.17310)**: training-free "Activation Memory Bank" with stored keys, aimed at long-context understanding. Overlap: training-free activation bank. Difference: per the snippet, dense activation reuse inside a long context, not binary keys, not a logit-side error memory. **[snippet]** — [arXiv 2506.17310](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.17310)
- **NGM: A Plug-and-Play Training-Free Memory Module for LLMs (arXiv 2605.16893)**: training-free memory for frozen decoder-only models; builds N-gram representations from pretrained token embeddings by parameter-free pooling, similarity retrieval, cosine gating. Difference: keys are pooled token embeddings (not neuron states), gated, no error term. **[snippet]** — [arXiv 2605.16893](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.16893)
- Binary/Hamming retrieval from network activations exists for image hashing only in what surfaced (e.g. arXiv 1607.05140), i.e. learned hash codes, not a frozen LM's own gate pattern. **[snippet]** — [arXiv 1607.05140](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1607.05140)

### Inferences
- The binary route key is the single most distinctive element of the method relative to everything found; the NPK paper is the citation a reviewer will expect, because the route-similarity kernel is essentially an NPK-style gate-overlap kernel applied to a transformer's MLP gates.

### Gaps
- Queries run with no direct hit: "neuron activation pattern binary keys memory retrieval frozen LLM training-free inference-time learning logits"; ""activation pattern" OR "gate pattern" OR "neural path" kernel ReLU on/off pattern similarity nearest neighbor memory language model "neural path kernel" kNN"; ""MLP activation" OR "neuron activation" "binary" code "Hamming" similarity retrieval "kNN-LM" OR "datastore" OR "memory" language model next-token frozen hashing activation sign pattern key".
- Not searched: OpenReview full-text for ICLR/NeurIPS/ICML 2026 submissions; sparse-autoencoder-feature keyed retrieval (SAE-Lens based) as a near cousin of binary neuron keys; kNN-LM datastore compression by sign-binarising hidden states (plausible to exist, would be binarised hidden states rather than MLP gates).

## Pairing (B): stored prediction error (told minus the model's own stored prediction) added to logits by similarity on a frozen LLM

### Takeaway
Not found in exact form. The dual-form algebra is published (Fast Weight Layers, 2022) but meta-trained and in-sequence; the frozen-model logit-correction work found is either a trained parametric module or a targets-only datastore; no "residual datastore" or "error-correcting kNN-LM" surfaced.

### Cited Findings
- **Fast Weight Layers (arXiv 2212.02475)**: gradient updates of dynamic evaluation expressed as linear attention; meta-learned. Closest published statement of "gradient step kept as attention over stored tokens instead of a weight change". **[fetched, abstract only]** — [arXiv 2212.02475](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.02475)
- **Safe Error Correction for Language Models: Frozen-Base Adjustment with Capability Preservation (Gautam Kishore, arXiv 2609.16145, 14 Sep 2026)**: a "lightweight logit-level correction module" (about 34M parameters) that "sits atop a fully frozen Gemma 4 E2B model"; "only the correction module learns, via supervised fine-tuning followed by reference-free DPO" on 83,400 error-correction pairs; KL preservation term. Overlap: frozen base, correction at the logits, error-driven. Difference: trained parametric module, no retrieval, no stored per-token error. **[fetched, abstract only]** — [arXiv 2609.16145](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16145v1)
- **OCRR: A Benchmark for Online Correction Recovery under Distribution Shift (Adrian Grassi, arXiv 2605.03153, 4 May 2026)**: benchmarks online-correction methods including kNN-LM (a datastore that grows with corrections while the parametric head stays frozen), EWC, A-GEM, LwF, LoRA on DeBERTa-v3-large and the author's "hash-chained append-only substrate" with a "margin-band majority vote". Overlap: corrections stored non-parametrically beside a frozen head. Difference: classification-style benchmark; nothing in the abstract stores (target - prediction) or adds it to logits by similarity. **[fetched, abstract only]** — [arXiv 2605.03153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03153)
- **Frustratingly Simple Black-Box Adaptation of Language Models via Logit Bias (arXiv 2607.22837)**: learns one fixed bias vector added to the logits at every decoding step, weights unchanged. Overlap: additive logit-side adaptation of a frozen model. Difference: a single learned static vector, not retrieved, not per-token error. **[snippet]** — [arXiv 2607.22837](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22837)
- **Enabling Robust In-Context Memory and Rapid Task Adaptation in Transformers with Hebbian and Gradient-Based Plasticity (arXiv 2510.21908)**: adds fast weights to a decoder-only transformer that evolve along the sequence "without gradient updates on the static parameters". Difference: the plasticity rules are part of a trained model, fast weights have the shape of the static weights (primal form), not a frozen off-the-shelf LLM. **[snippet]** — [arXiv 2510.21908](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.21908)
- **Titans (NeurIPS 2025)**: memory module updated at inference by a gradient-based "surprise" measure with momentum and forgetting; the module and its update rule are part of pretraining. A reimplementation study notes the original "did not isolate the contribution of test-time updates". Overlap: surprise as the write signal. Difference: trained architecture, real weight updates to a memory MLP, not logit-side, not a frozen off-the-shelf model. **[snippet]** — [NeurIPS paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/a4ca07aa108036f80cbb5b82285fd4b1-Paper-Conference.pdf), [Titans Revisited, arXiv 2510.09551](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.09551v1)
- **Learning without training: The implicit dynamics of in-context learning (Dherin, Munn, Mazzawi, Wunder, Gonzalvo; Google; arXiv 2507.16003, July 2025)** and follow-up **arXiv 2511.17864**: a forward pass with context equals a pass without context but with a low-rank update to the MLP weights. Overlap: "context acts as an implicit weight update, no training" framing, which is the closest published language to the method's dual-form claim. Difference: a theoretical equivalence about what attention already does inside the context window; the implicit updates "depend on the input query tokens"; no external store, no persistence beyond the context. **[snippet]** — [arXiv 2507.16003](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.16003v3), [arXiv 2511.17864](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.17864)

### Inferences
- Storing (told - predicted) rather than told is exactly what distinguishes a dual-form gradient step from kNN-LM; since FWL already wrote the last-layer gradient as attention, the honest description is "a persistent, untrained, cross-prompt Fast Weight Layer with a different kernel", and the novelty question for (B) is narrow.
- The stated property "places the model already predicted contribute zero" is the delta-rule property; Titans-style "surprise" is the same idea realised as trained weight updates.

### Gaps
- Queries run with no direct hit: "kNN-LM error correction datastore store residual between target and model prediction logits frozen language model"; "dynamic evaluation without weight updates dual form fast weights in-context gradient descent last layer memory transformer inference 2025"; "training-free test-time memory frozen LLM "surprise" OR "prediction error" stored applied to output logits associative memory Hebbian delta rule 2025 2026 arXiv".
- **[memory, unverified this session]** Irie, Csordas and Schmidhuber, "The Dual Form of Neural Networks Revisited" (ICML 2022, arXiv 2202.05798) shows a linear layer trained by gradient descent equals a key-value attention over all training inputs weighted by their error signals. If confirmed it is the most exact prior statement of the algebra in step 3 and must be cited. Likewise Krause et al. dynamic evaluation (arXiv 1709.07432) and Grave et al. neural cache (arXiv 1612.04426) are the classic ancestors. None of these three was opened this session.
- Not checked: kNN-MT variants that store model confidence or train on residuals, and "residual kNN" in tabular/vision literature (kNN on residuals of a base model is a classic boosting-style idea and may exist under another name).

## Pairing (C): sequence-carried / alignment-style similarity as the retrieval kernel

### Takeaway
Partial neighbours only. RetoMaton follows pointers between consecutive datastore entries and EM-LLM retrieves contiguous events, but no surfaced work uses an ungated soft recurrence that multiplies current similarity by one plus the previous step's weight at the previous stored position.

### Cited Findings
- **RetoMaton (arXiv 2201.12431)**: "saved pointers between consecutive datastore entries", clustering of entries into automaton states, transition weights from negative squared L2 distance between current and stored hidden states; the model either does a fresh kNN search or continues from previously retrieved neighbours. Overlap: exploits the sequential order of the stored pass. Difference: hard pointer traversal plus restarts (a gate/reset in effect), versus the method's ungated soft accumulation over all places. **[snippet]** — [arXiv 2201.12431](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.12431)
- **EM-LLM (github.com/em-llm/EM-LLM-model)**: human-episodic-memory-inspired memory for LLMs with "no fine-tuning"; segments the stream into events and retrieves by k-NN plus "selecting contiguous events". Overlap: frozen model, temporal contiguity in retrieval. Difference: retrieved events are placed back into the model's context (retrieval into context), not a logit-side kernel; contiguity is at event level, not a per-token alignment recurrence. **[snippet]** — [GitHub em-llm](https://github.com/em-llm/EM-LLM-model)
- **Nearest Neighbor Speculative Decoding, NEST (arXiv 2405.19325, NeurIPS 2024)**: kNN-LM descendant with a per-step adaptive mixing coefficient from retrieval confidence and span continuation from the datastore. Overlap: continues along stored spans. Difference: gated/accept-reject span copying and a normalised mixture. **[snippet]** — [arXiv 2405.19325](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.19325)

### Inferences
- The recurrence P_t[n] = sim^scale * (1 + P_(t-1)[n-1]) is a soft local-alignment (Smith-Waterman-like, diagonal-only, no gap) score; that framing, applied as an LM memory kernel, did not surface, but the idea "a match that continues a previous match counts more" is established by RetoMaton/NEST and should be cited as the ancestor.

### Gaps
- Query run with no direct hit: "kNN-LM retrieval consecutive datastore entries alignment score accumulate previous neighbor match "continuation" span-level soft alignment copy generator suffix automaton unlimiformer 2023 2024 2025"; plus "RetoMaton automaton pointers kNN-LM sequence continuation retrieval; "kNN-LM" temporal alignment carried similarity".
- Not checked: Copy-Generator / "Copy is All You Need" (phrase-level retrieval), suffix-automaton or infinite-gram models (unbounded n-gram match length is an exact-match analogue of carried alignment), and speech/DTW-style alignment memories.

## Pairing (D): products/projects claiming a frozen model "learns" from prompts via activation recordings; open-source implementations

### Takeaway
No product or repository surfaced that stores neuron firing patterns as an inference-time memory for a local LLM. The closest in claim language are the Oct 2026 activation-steering-memory preprint and Google's "Learning without training" theory; open-source hits are RAG-style memory, model inspection or weight editing.

### Inferences
- The open field around (D) is crowded with "frozen LLM + memory" claims, but they split into text retrieval, trained adapters, steering vectors, or real test-time weight updates; a per-token, logit-side, error-valued, activation-keyed store sits in none of those bins.
- Two 2026 papers (2610.33025, 2607.00368) set the evaluation bar: perplexity gains from test-time adaptation do not imply recall of facts, so any claim that the model "learns what it is told" needs behavioural recall tests, not loss.
